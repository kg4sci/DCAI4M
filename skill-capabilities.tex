\section{Overview}
% =====================================================================
The survey's quantitative evaluation is produced by a suite of two
cooperating agent skills stored under \texttt{.qoder/skills/}, plus a
deterministic figure generator:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{materials-benchmark-scoring} --- the rubric engine. It scores
    any Excel workbook of materials-domain benchmarks (one benchmark per row)
    against a two-axis rubric and writes the scores back to a new workbook.
  \item \textbf{online-benchmark-scoring} --- the online driver. It reads the
    community-maintained benchmark ledger (a Kingsoft Docs / kdocs online
    workbook with three task-family sheets, whose share URL is kept in the
    plain-text file \texttt{openxlsx}), determines which papers are already
    scored, and scores only the new ones by delegating to the rubric engine.
  \item \textbf{summary/make\_main\_figures.py} --- regenerates the two main
    figures of the survey directly from the scored registry, so figures and
    scores can never drift apart.
\end{enumerate}

Four design principles govern the whole suite:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Evidence-only scoring.} A dimension is scored only if the paper's
    full text (or, failing that, the ledger row) explicitly supports it;
    buzzwords such as ``agent'' or ``closed-loop'' never raise a score by
    themselves, and claims are distinguished from executed demonstrations.
  \item \emph{Deterministic aggregation.} Weighted sums, caps, level bands and
    the $S$ total are computed by script (\texttt{compute\_scores.py}); the
    agent never performs the arithmetic by hand.
  \item \emph{Local-only outputs.} The online document is treated as read-only;
    all artifacts land under \texttt{summary/} in the workspace.
  \item \emph{Incremental and resumable.} A local registry ledger records every
    scored paper, so repeated runs score only newly added rows and earlier
    results are never recomputed or overwritten.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Rubric engine: \texttt{materials-benchmark-scoring}}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The two-axis rubric}
Each benchmark receives an \emph{intelligence} score (axis~X) and an
\emph{experimental preparation guidance} score (axis~Y).

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\caption{Axis~X: six weighted intelligence dimensions, each scored 0--4.}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cll@{}}
\toprule
Dim. & Meaning & Weight \\
\midrule
O & Open-endedness (open, non-enumerable output space) & 25\% \\
A & Autonomy (one-shot assistance $\rightarrow$ full autonomy) & 20\% \\
I & Iteration \& feedback (single shot $\rightarrow$ long-horizon loop) & 20\% \\
G & Generalisation across systems / domains / modalities & 15\% \\
U & Uncertainty (calibrated and decision-used) & 10\% \\
P & Process \& executability (executable, reusable artifacts) & 10\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The aggregation is
$X_{100} = \sum_d w_d \cdot \frac{s_d}{4} \cdot 100$ and
$X_5 = X_{100}/20 \in [0,5]$, mapped to levels L0--L5
(symbolic $\rightarrow$ autonomous discovery). Level~5 carries an additional
gate ($O=4 \wedge A\geq 3 \wedge I=4 \wedge P\geq 3$); failing it demotes the
row to Level~4 and the demotion is recorded.
Axis~Y sums three dimensions $E$ (experimental grounding), $R$ (real-world
realism) and $C$ (closed-loop readiness), each 0--2, to
$S = E+R+C \in [0,6]$. A dimension that the paper neither reports nor applies
is marked \texttt{NR}/\texttt{NA} and is never imputed; in that case $S$ is
reported as not reportable.

\subsection{Anti-inflation mechanisms}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Scoring-time caps:} no executed agent/closed loop $\Rightarrow
    P\leq 1, A\leq 1$; generation judged only by validity/novelty/diversity
    $\Rightarrow O\leq 2$ and $X_5\leq 2.5$; claimed but undemonstrated
    feedback $\Rightarrow A\leq 2$.
  \item \emph{Post-aggregation caps (script-enforced):} $O<4 \Rightarrow
    X_5\leq 2.5$; $A<3 \Rightarrow X_5\leq 3.0$; $I<3 \Rightarrow X_5\leq 3.0$.
  \item \emph{Per-dimension confidence} (high/medium/low) is recorded with
    quoted evidence for every dimension, and low-confidence or incomplete rows
    are flagged in the final report.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scripts and I/O contract}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \texttt{read\_benchmarks.py} --- workbook $\rightarrow$ JSON rows with
    1-based \texttt{row\_index} for later alignment; \texttt{--preview} mode
    inspects headers before scoring.
  \item \texttt{compute\_scores.py} --- adds \texttt{x100\_raw},
    \texttt{x5\_raw}, \texttt{x5\_final}, \texttt{applied\_caps},
    \texttt{level}, \texttt{level\_label}, \texttt{y\_total},
    \texttt{y\_complete}; enforces all numeric caps and the Level-5 gate.
  \item \texttt{write\_scores.py} --- copies the original workbook and appends
    the score columns; existing cells are never overwritten.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Online driver: \texttt{online-benchmark-scoring}}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Reading a canvas-rendered online workbook}
The kdocs grid is canvas-rendered, so plain HTTP fetching returns nothing.
The skill drives a real browser (browser-use MCP): it tolerates the
single-sign-on bounce to a read-only view, queries the workbook's internal
JavaScript model for sheet names and cell strings, and---because only the
active sheet resides in memory---activates each tab with a synthetic click
before dumping it. Large grids are posted straight from the browser to a
local HTTP receiver (\texttt{local\_receiver.py} on \texttt{127.0.0.1:8765})
that writes \texttt{summary/raw/<sheet>.json}, avoiding any round-trip of
tens of kilobytes through the conversation. If the browser or the login wall
blocks access, the skill falls back to a user-exported local \texttt{.xlsx}.

\subsection{Incremental scored-state ledger}
\texttt{registry.py} maintains \texttt{summary/scored\_registry.json}, keyed by
normalized DOI (or title when the DOI is empty). A paper is ``scored'' iff its
key exists; each entry stores \texttt{scored\_at}, \texttt{x5\_final},
\texttt{level}, \texttt{y\_total} and \texttt{evidence\_source}. The
\texttt{unscored} subcommand diffs the current online snapshot against the
ledger, and \texttt{add} records newly scored rows---making every run
incremental and safely interruptible.

\subsection{Evidence hierarchy}
For each unscored row the skill first attempts the full text via web search
and fetch (arXiv, OpenReview, publisher DOI pages) and records
\texttt{evidence\_source = "fulltext"}; otherwise it scores from the ledger's
own fields (task code, input/output, modality, data source, scale, metrics,
baselines, strengths, notes) with
\texttt{evidence\_source = "table"} and reduced confidence. Content appearing
in neither source is never invented.

\subsection{Pipeline and outputs}
The ten-step pipeline (resolve URL $\rightarrow$ open doc $\rightarrow$ list
sheets $\rightarrow$ dump grids $\rightarrow$ build \texttt{.xlsx} snapshots
$\rightarrow$ diff registry $\rightarrow$ gather evidence $\rightarrow$ score
and aggregate $\rightarrow$ write scored workbooks and update registry
$\rightarrow$ merge summary) produces:
\texttt{summary/raw/} (browser-extracted grids), \texttt{summary/snapshot/}
(local workbook mirrors), \texttt{summary/scored/} (per-sheet scores,
computed aggregates and scored workbooks), \texttt{summary/scored\_registry.json}
and \texttt{summary/summary.md} (consolidated report with confidence,
completeness and cap flags).

\section{Figure generation}
% =====================================================================
\texttt{summary/make\_main\_figures.py} renders the two main figures of the
survey from \texttt{summary/scored/*.computed.json} (PNG at 300\,dpi plus
vector PDF into \texttt{summary/figures/}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Fig.\,1, scoring framework:} the two-axis rubric as a schematic
    (dimension cards with weights, aggregation formulas, mandatory caps,
    L0--L5 level ladder with the Level-5 gate, NR/NA rule, and the
    sheet$\rightarrow$evidence$\rightarrow$score$\rightarrow$aggregate
    $\rightarrow$map pipeline).
  \item \emph{Fig.\,2, quantitative landscape:} every scored benchmark placed
    at $(X_5, S)$, with L0--L5 band shading, a per-level marginal histogram,
    task-family colours, an open-marker strip for benchmarks whose $S$ is not
    reportable, the emerging-frontier region (agent-grade intelligence with
    experiment-grounded closed loops), and an auto-computed take-home message.
\end{itemize}
Because the script reads the scored registry, re-running it after any future
scoring pass updates both figures automatically.

\section{Usage and limitations}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{Triggers.} Natural-language requests such as ``read the online
sheet in \texttt{openxlsx} and score the unscored papers'', ``score the
benchmarks in this Excel with x.md/y.md'', or ``regenerate the survey
figures'' invoke the corresponding component; no manual scripting is needed.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Rows scored from table fields only carry reduced
per-dimension confidence and are flagged as such; the online reader depends
on the kdocs internal JavaScript model (re-verification is needed if the
vendor changes it); and the rubric intentionally scores the \emph{task} a
benchmark poses, not the predictive accuracy or writing quality of the paper
that introduces it.
