## P · Autonomy & Actionability

**Score**: 3; scored
**Criterion**: Automatically selects the next action based on feedback. 
**Rationale**: Researchers set material objectives, initialization methods, and acquisition rules; the program then automatically selects x* each round based on surrogate predictions and revealed observations, reads labels, updates the set and model, and repeats. Therefore it automatically selects the next action based on feedback, exceeding merely recommending or only executing a preset fixed candidate order.
**Level comparison**: Level 2 "Automatically executes a fixed workflow." can describe the overall control skeleton; level 3 "Automatically selects the next action based on feedback." more specifically describes the x* decided by the updated model each round. Level 4 "Autonomously plans, executes, monitors, and corrects the whole process." cannot be automatically inferred from loop point selection, and in particular the physical capability of the original robot data collection cannot be moved into this benchmark.
**Task**: T-CB
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Roles are clearly distinguished: researchers set objectives and rules, BO proposes and executes point selection and label queries in computational replay, and the model updates using feedback; label queries are not called new physical experiment execution. A fixed acquisition rule does not equal the candidate action order being pre-fixed, so level 3 has direct support.
## E · Dependence on Experimental Results

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: Real experimental results serve as formal testing; performance can be independently evaluated on experimental data. 
**Rationale**: The mechanical toughness of Crossed barrel comes from grid experiments by a robot system on actual 3D-printed structures. This paper composes these measurements into a discrete ground-truth pool, conducts preset retrospective evaluation of the speed at which BO discovers the top-5% toughness structures, and separately reports Crossed barrel's Top%, EF, and AF. Therefore historical experimental results are used both for model updating and directly determine the quality candidates and discovery performance in formal evaluation. What is simulated is the sampling order, not testing only with simulation or proxy labels; nor is it merely a few success cases.
**Level comparison**: Level 0 requires "No real experimental results; evaluation uses only simulation or proxy labels.", which does not fit E-1 and E-2's historical experimental ground truth; level 1 requires "Experimental results used for training, calibration, or few-shot examples, but do not constitute formal experimental testing.", while E-3 and E-4 show clear experimental-pool discovery evaluation and separate reporting; therefore level 2 fits. Per the original criterion, historical experimental measurements are valid and authors need not redo experiments; this is also not merely reporting an inseparable experimental/simulation mixed score.
**Task**: T-CB
**Boundary & dispute notes**: A possible dispute is misreading "simulate" in the text as using only simulation labels, or misreading per-round training on known samples as experiments used only for training. E-1 and E-2 establish the labels as historical experimental ground truth, and E-3 and E-4 establish that real high-quality candidates are used for formal discovery performance and separately reported, so level 2 is adopted.
