## Initial Scoring, Independent Review & Final Adjudication

## O · Output & Decision Role

**Score**: 4; scored
**Criterion**: Decisions about the next candidates, experiments, resource allocation, or discovery strategy. 
**Rationale**: Task 1's query policy decides the next batch of MXenes to evaluate from an unlabeled pool; band-gap prediction and ranking serve this action selection. Task 2's maximization or future inverse design is not counted.
**Level comparison**: Level 2's candidate ranking is indeed an internal step, but level 4 more accurately describes the next-candidate query decision required by the formal task; level 3's generation or inverse design is not this task.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Looking only at the proxy model would fall into the prediction or ranking level; the source's formalized workflow requires the strategy to select the next batch of candidates, so task-level decision-making prevails.
## A · Adaptivity & Sequentiality

**Score**: pending (candidate 3); pending (with candidate score)
**Criterion**: Insufficient information; no numeric level selected for now
**Rationale**: Task 1 is not one-shot screening: labels obtained from queries change the subsequent training set and selection. However, levels 3 and 4 have a not-yet-resolved semantic overlap for the closed loop formed by historical label replay.
**Level comparison**: Level 0's "Completely static, one-shot prediction", level 1's "Conditional prediction or generation without updating behavior based on new results", and level 2's "One-time active selection" all do not fit. Level 3's "Multi-round sequential optimization using simulation or offline feedback" directly fits historical label replay; level 4's "Multi-round, feedback-driven adaptive closed loop" may also describe the same prediction-query-retraining loop, and physical experiment cannot be added on our own as a necessary condition for level 4.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Candidate 3 adopts the clearest qualification, the feedback source; if "adaptive closed loop" is understood as the algorithm's internal loop, the same evidence also supports 4, so no formal score is released.
## I · Workflow Integration

**Score**: 3; scored
**Criterion**: Multiple models or modules connected in sequence. 
**Rationale**: Task 1 actually chains the surrogate model, query policy, label revelation, and retraining, belonging to multiple modules in the same discovery task. The paper does not implement candidate generation or experiment planning; experimental workflow integration is listed as future work.
**Level comparison**: It is above level 2's enhanced single-task version, because prediction, policy querying, and retraining are actually connected in sequence. Although level 4 lists stages such as prediction, screening, and feedback, its integrated discovery system also involves a more complete discovery process; this paper has no implemented generation or experiment planning, and experimental integration is explicitly future work.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Another interpretation might directly call prediction, querying, and feedback an integrated discovery system; here, according to level 4's wording on more complete discovery stages and the paper's future-work qualification, level 3 is adopted, left for independent review.
## G · Goal & Constraint Handling

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: A single objective with static constraints. 
**Rationale**: Task 1's objective is to efficiently discover candidates within a fixed band-gap window; the target window and query budget are given in advance. Multiple policy metrics and surrogate model training losses are not task-level joint multi-objectives.
**Level comparison**: A single objective plus a fixed band-gap window and query budget is more specific than level 1; level 3's multi-objective or multi-constraint is not implemented, and the paper lists multi-objective as future work. Level 4's dynamic multi-objective resource decision-making also has no corresponding workflow.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: If the target window is treated only as a success definition, there is still a fixed query budget as a task execution constraint; therefore level 2 is adopted, with its constraint scope clarified.
## U · Uncertainty & Failure Handling

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: Uncertainty is used in candidate selection. 
**Rationale**: The uncertainty, entropy, and UCB policies compared in Task 1 use candidate-level predictive uncertainty for query selection. The paper does not give a task-level handling mechanism for noise, failures, or negative results, nor use uncertainty for risk-aware objectives.
**Level comparison**: Unlike level 1 only reporting basic uncertainty, candidate uncertainty directly enters the query policy; level 3 requires explicitly handling noise, failures, or negative results, and level 4 requires risk-aware search, while the described policy aims at exploration and discovery efficiency.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: UCB's variance term cannot automatically be interpreted as experimental failure risk; level 2 is adopted according to its actual role.
## P · Autonomy & Actionability

**Score**: 3; scored
**Criterion**: Automatically selects the next action based on feedback. 
**Rationale**: Humans set the target window and budget; the policy selects query materials each round based on the current model, and continues selecting after labels are revealed and the training set updated. The automation scope is limited to the query workflow of historical label replay; whole-experiment autonomous planning and error correction are future work.
**Level comparison**: It exceeds level 2's mere Automatically executes a fixed workflow, because each round updates the model based on newly revealed labels and selects the next candidate; it does not reach level 4's autonomous planning, execution, monitoring, and correction of the whole discovery process, with experimental integration explicitly future work.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Offline feedback can still drive the algorithm to select the next action; the level-3 judgment is limited to the implemented query strategy, without expanding it into level-4 full-process autonomy.
## E · Dependence on Experimental Results

**Score**: 0; scored
**Criterion**: No real experimental results; evaluation uses only simulation or proxy labels. 
**Rationale**: Task 1's target labels are PBE0 computed band gaps, with data from existing slab-DFT studies. What is revealed at query time is pre-stored computational labels, simulating new DFT evaluation. The paper lists experimental workflow integration as future work. The described training, query feedback, and formal comparison are all based on computational labels, with no experimental measurement test results.
**Level comparison**: Level 0 fits PBE0 computational labels and retrospective DFT replay; level 1 requires experimental results entering training, calibration, or cases, and level 2 requires experimental results constituting a separately assessable formal test, neither fitting this paper's reported task 1 data source and evaluation.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The photovoltaic-use wording does not change the computational source of the band-gap labels; background mention of experiments or future connection to experimental workflows also does not constitute an experimental test.
## R · Alignment with Experimental Needs

**Score**: 1; scored
**Criterion**: Data explicitly targets an experimental need but does not cover key constraints required to fulfill it. 
**Rationale**: Fixed question one: yes, the paper explicitly maps the target band-gap window to photovoltaic absorber and device-compatible band-gap screening. Fixed question two: no, Task 1 only defines successful candidates by the PBE0 band-gap window; the authors list stability and synthesizability studies of discovered candidates as future work. This supports experimental-need alignment but does not yet cover the key constraints of that use.
**Level comparison**: Level 0's "no connection to a specific experimental use" does not fit the explicit photovoltaic absorber and device-compatible screening use. Level 2 requires related objectives and key constraints both covered by data or candidate scope; the formal objective is only a computational band-gap window, with stability and synthesizability left to future work, so level 1 best fits.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The design space lists composition and structure variations, but these ranges themselves do not guarantee candidates are stable, synthesizable, or device-applicable; the authors explicitly leave the first two to future research.
