**Task**: T-SUITE
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Multiple single-objective subtasks in parallel alone are insufficient to prove multi-objective; here there is additional direct evidence of a photovoltaic combination function and an OLED third combination objective, so level 3 is chosen.
## U · Uncertainty & Failure Handling

**Score** 2(Expert review)
**Criterion**: Noise, failures, and negative results are explicitly handled. 
**Rationale**: Reaction substrate simulation may produce seemingly excellent anomalous properties due to conformational search or transition-state optimization failure; the paper explicitly implements outlier detection and assigns very low fitness, so failed results are not mistakenly selected. This mechanism only applies to reaction substrate instances and cannot be interpreted as probabilistic predictive uncertainty or risk-aware search.
**Level comparison**: Level 0 cannot summarize the implemented failed-result detection and penalty. Level 1's basic uncertainty reporting and level 2's uncertainty screening both differ from this evidence. Level 3's failure handling is directly supported by outlier detection and low fitness; level 4 requires uncertainty-driven risk-aware search, which the source does not show.
**Task**: T-SUITE
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Level 3 lists noise, failures, and negative results; what is currently directly confirmed is computational failure and its outlier penalty, which cannot be extrapolated to experimental negative results or risk-aware search.
## P · Autonomy & Actionability

**Criterion**: Automatically selects the next action based on feedback. 
**Rationale**: The objective is preset by the benchmark; the evaluated generative model proposes molecules, and the property simulation workflow executes evaluation. The formally compared LSTM-HC automatically selects the top two after each round's evaluation as the next round's input and retrains, constituting feedback-based next-step action selection; the paper does not describe autonomously planning, executing, and correcting the whole experimental discovery process.
**Level comparison**: Level 0's prediction-only output does not fit the formal generation optimization workflow; level 1's human-decided next action is insufficient to describe the baseline's automatic seed selection; level 2's fixed workflow does exist, but the evaluated LSTM-HC selects the next round's input based on evaluation feedback, satisfying level 3. Level 4's autonomous planning, execution, monitoring, and correction of the whole process has no corresponding implemented evidence.
**Task**: T-SUITE
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Treating TARTARUS only as scoring software would underestimate the formally evaluated feedback seed selection; this judgment also limits that capability to the relevant baselines only.

## R · Alignment with Experimental Needs

**Score**: 1; scored
**Criterion**: Data explicitly targets an experimental need but does not cover key constraints required to fulfill it. 
**Rationale**: Fixed question one is yes: the photovoltaic benchmark is explicitly designed for device molecules with specified donor-acceptor combinations, and other domains also declare OLED, ligand, and reaction substrate uses. Fixed question two cannot be judged yes for the whole suite: the authors explicitly state the ligand benchmark does not consider toxicity, solubility, stability, and other properties that determine whether candidates are valid; emitter stability needs additional screening. The existing structural constraints and objectives only show some practical limits are incorporated, not making the whole suite satisfy key-constraint completeness.
**Level comparison**: Level 0 requires the objective unconnected to a specific experimental use, which the explicit photovoltaic device pairing use does not fit. Level 1 corresponds to a clear use but lacking key constraints; the ligand task explicitly discards properties such as toxicity, solubility, and stability, and the emitters also acknowledge needing stronger screening. Level 2 requires data and candidate scope jointly supporting a clearly defined experimental decision and incorporating its key constraints; some domains have hard constraints, but this cannot eliminate the clear gaps in other formal domains.
**Task**: T-SUITE
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The reaction-substrate task already has structure cores, reactive-group and SAscore limits, and the ligands also have structure filtering, so some subtasks may be closer to level 2; the selected object is the full suite, and the different constraints across domains cannot be assembled into one complete experimental decision.
