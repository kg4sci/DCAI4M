## O · Output & Decision Role

**Score**: 4; scored
**Criterion**: Decisions about the next candidates, experiments, resource allocation, or discovery strategy. 
**Rationale**: The formal planning task requires the algorithm to give the next query point in parameter space; this point decides the query for the next simulated experiment response. The simulator's property prediction is an environment response, not the task output evaluated by this item.
**Level comparison**: Levels 1 and 2 describe property prediction or ranking, neither being the planner's required output. Level 3's candidate generation can describe its action, but level 4 precisely requires deciding the next candidates or experiments, so 4 is chosen.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The evaluation object is the planning task T1; the simulator's standalone prediction output is not used as O's upper bound.
## A · Adaptivity & Sequentiality

**Score**: 4; scored
**Criterion**: Multi-round, feedback-driven adaptive closed loop. 
**Rationale**: The supported Bayesian planner updates the surrogate model with new data and selects candidate points based on the model; the framework connects the planner and simulator, runs multi-round optimization, and the planner retains historical parameters and responses. The random search baseline executed by the paper does not use feedback, and its behavior cannot represent all supported planners.
**Level comparison**: Level 3 explicitly covers multi-round sequential optimization under simulation feedback; the supported Phoenics planner also uses historical observations in repeated queries, updates the model, and decides the next point, forming a feedback-driven simulation closed loop, so level 4 also holds. Level 4's source does not require physical experiments, and that threshold cannot be added on our own.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: It might be judged static based only on the random baseline in Section 5; the frozen object also includes the explicitly supported Bayesian planning task and the multi-round simulation activity in Section 4.3, so that baseline cannot limit the entire task.
## I · Workflow Integration

## G · Goal & Constraint Handling

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: A single objective with static constraints. 
**Rationale**: Each core dataset has one target property, and the planner can set a minimization objective; when executing random search, parameters are sampled from their respective allowed ranges. The allowed ranges constitute static constraints of the formal candidate domain, not training losses or post-hoc statistical metrics.
**Level comparison**: Level 1 covers a single objective; level 2 further requires static constraints, satisfied by the allowed parameter range of formal candidates. Multiple variables each having boundaries belong to the same fixed feasible domain, and the source does not establish multi-objective or additional independent constraint optimization, so levels 3 and 4 are not chosen.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The multi-objective prior work cited in the source, the simulator output-layer positivity constraint, and the reported metrics do not belong to T1's joint objectives or additional decision constraints.
## U · Uncertainty & Failure Handling

**Score** 2(Expert review)
**Criterion**: Basic uncertainty reported but does not affect the strategy. 
**Rationale**: The BNN simulator explicitly models the target value for a given input as a distribution and can draw predictive samples to simulate noise. The random search baseline does not use historical feedback; the supplied source does not establish rules for the planner to screen by candidate-level uncertainty or adjust search by risk.
**Level comparison**: Level 0's purely deterministic output does not fit the probabilistic simulator. Level 1 is supported by the conditional target distribution and noisy response; levels 2 and 4 require uncertainty to directly change candidate selection or risk strategy, which these evidence do not establish. Although level 3 involves noise, the source does not prove explicit handling of failures and negative results.
**Task**: T1
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Between-run distributions and simulator prediction distributions are different quantities; this item relies only on the latter, and qualifies that the random baseline's strategy does not use historical results.
