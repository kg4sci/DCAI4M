## Initial Scoring, Independent Review & Final Adjudication

## O · Output & Decision Role

**Score**: 4; scored
**Criterion**: Decisions about the next candidates, experiments, resource allocation, or discovery strategy. 
**Rationale**: The formal task requires the acquisition function to consider cost and select the candidate input and fidelity for the next query; this is a next-step candidate and query-resource decision.
**Level comparison**: Level 2's candidate ranking is insufficient to describe selecting the next query; level 3 requires generation or inverse design, while here candidates are selected within a given space. Level 4 directly corresponds to the next-candidate and fidelity decision.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: A fixed candidate space does not preclude level 4; this level explicitly includes making decisions about the next candidates.
## A · Adaptivity & Sequentiality

**Score**: 4; scored
**Criterion**: Multi-round, feedback-driven adaptive closed loop. 
**Rationale**: The acquisition function uses progressively obtained data to train the surrogate and select the next query; the authors explicitly call the data acquisition sequential. The closed loop occurs within the benchmark optimization run, and feedback can come from pre-stored labels or a simulation function.
**Level comparison**: Level 3's "Multi-round sequential optimization using simulation or offline feedback." also describes the data source; level 4's "Multi-round, feedback-driven adaptive closed loop." further describes the behavioral mechanism. Sampled data changes the next query and runs sequentially, so level 4 is adopted; the original anchor does not stipulate that new physical experiments must be conducted.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The offline data source makes level 3 equally applicable, but sampled data drive the next round's selection, so level 4 is more specific; this does not indicate a physical-experiment autonomous closed loop.
## I · Workflow Integration

**Score**: 3; scored
**Criterion**: Multiple models or modules connected in sequence. 
**Rationale**: The actual optimization connects the cross-fidelity Gaussian process surrogate and cost-aware EI/MES acquisition modules in sequence, then enters the next round of queries. This is module chaining within the same optimization task, not forming a system covering multiple discovery stages such as generation and experiment execution.
**Level comparison**: Level 2's "An enhanced version of a single task." can describe the multi-fidelity extension relative to single fidelity; level 3 more directly describes the implemented sequential connection of the GP surrogate and acquisition function. Level 4 requires an integrated system of multiple discovery stages, and the source does not provide corresponding chains such as generation, experiment execution, or experiment planning.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Both levels 2 and 3 have reasonable interpretations; this judgment chooses level 3 per the source's "module", and clarifies its hierarchy is within the same optimization task, for subsequent focused review.
## G · Goal & Constraint Handling

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: A single objective with static constraints. 
**Rationale**: The actual optimization targets high-fidelity properties, the acquisition function adjusts utility by fixed-fidelity query cost, and runs under a preset total budget. Cost and budget are static constraints taking effect during execution; regret, discount, and information quantity are evaluation quantities, not constituting joint multi-objective optimization.
**Level comparison**: Level 1 has only a single objective, not reflecting the cost and budget taking effect during execution. Level 3 needs multiple objectives or multiple constraints; the cost weight and total budget belong to the same resource limit, and the source does not establish another independent optimization objective or constraint. Level 4 additionally needs dynamic, multi-objective decision-making with resource constraints.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Cost can be written both as a scaling term of acquisition utility and embodied in a fixed budget; interpreted as a static resource limit effective at execution time, level 2.
## U · Uncertainty & Failure Handling

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: Uncertainty is used in candidate selection. 
**Rationale**: EI prefers high or predictively uncertain candidates, and MES uses expected high-fidelity entropy reduction to select the next query. Uncertainty directly affects candidate decisions. The repeated-run standard deviation is a reported statistic, and the -1 discount when the target regret value is not reached is a post-hoc evaluation rule.
**Level comparison**: Level 1 only requires reporting basic uncertainty; EI/MES uses it for query selection, fitting level 2. Level 3 requires Noise, failures, and negative results are explicitly handled, and the post-hoc discount failure rule is insufficient to prove the task policy has this handling mechanism; level 4's risk-aware search is also not established.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Setting the discount to -1 can be called failure evaluation, but it is not the noise, failure, and negative-result handling mechanism required by level 3; level 2 has more direct support.
## P · Autonomy & Actionability

**Score**: 3; scored
**Criterion**: Automatically selects the next action based on feedback. 
**Rationale**: The authors preset objectives and budgets, and the acquisition function in the BoTorch run automatically selects the next candidate and fidelity based on sampled data. The paper does not prove the system autonomously plans and executes a complete physical experimental process.
**Level comparison**: Level 2's Automatically executes a fixed workflow has been exceeded, because the next action changes with sampled data. Level 4 requires autonomously planning, executing, monitoring, and correcting the whole process; the text explicitly only provides optimized query selection, and some preceding evaluation remains future work.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: Level 2 also applies to an already automatically running fixed algorithm, but the next action is determined by per-round data, so level 3 is chosen.
## E · Dependence on Experimental Results

**Score**: 2; scored
**Criterion**: Real experimental results serve as formal testing; performance can be independently evaluated on experimental data. 
**Rationale**: FreeSolv and Alexandria respectively use experimental measurements as high-fidelity targets; the paper reports their optimization performance separately by benchmark case. Therefore experimental results enter formal evaluation and can be identified separately from the simulated high-fidelity COFs case. Measurements in historical databases satisfy the original criterion; the authors are not required to redo physical experiments.
**Level comparison**: Level 0 contradicts FreeSolv's and Alexandria's experimental high-fidelity labels; level 1 only applies when experimental values do not enter formal testing, while Figure 4 reports optimization performance case by case; so level 2 best fits the verified evidence.
**Task**: T-MFBO
**Boundary & dispute notes**: The suite mixes simulation and experimental high-fidelity cases; the original rubric allows performance to be evaluated separately on experimental data, and Figure 4's case-by-case results eliminate the concern of only having mixed aggregate metrics.
